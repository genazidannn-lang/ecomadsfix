How Google decides there's a mismatch

Googlebot opens the product's link URL, reads the price and availability from the visible page and from structured data (the Product markup in the page code), and compares them with price, sale_price and availability in the feed. The issue shows on the product as something like Mismatched value (page crawl) [price] or [availability].

Two things follow from that. First, Google sees the page as a visitor from wherever its crawler is, usually the US, not as you see it. Second, the structured data counts as much as the visible price.

Common causes of a price mismatch

CauseWhat happensFix
Feed updates less often than pricesYou changed prices in the morning, the feed is fetched once a day at night.Fetch more often, or connect the store through an app or the Merchant API that syncs changes right away.
Sale price on the site, not in the feedThe page shows €39 crossed out to €29, the feed sends only price = 39.Send sale_price and sale_price_effective_date, or keep the sale out of the feed entirely.
VATIn the EU and UK prices must include VAT. In the US they exclude tax. The feed sends a net price, the page shows gross, or the other way round.Match the rule for the target country on both sides.
Currency switched by locationShopify Markets or a currency app shows USD to the crawler while the feed says EUR.Separate URLs per market (subfolders or domains) and a feed per country pointing to them.
VariantsThe feed item is the XL in red at €45, the link opens the product with the default €39 variant selected.Link to the variant itself (on Shopify, ?variant=ID), so the page opens with that price.
Wrong structured dataThe theme outputs the lowest variant price, an old price or the wrong currency in Product markup.Check the markup with Google's Rich Results Test and fix the theme template.
Price only after an actionPrice appears after login, in the cart, or after closing a popup.Show the final price on the product page without any action.

Availability mismatch

Same mechanism, different field. The usual cases:

  • The feed says in_stock, the page says "Sold out" because the feed is a day behind.
  • The store keeps selling when stock is zero (on Shopify: "Continue selling when out of stock"), so the page shows "Add to cart" while the feed sends out_of_stock.
  • Pre-orders sent as in_stock. Use preorder or backorder together with availability_date.
  • The page's structured data has InStock hardcoded regardless of the real stock.

Automatic item updates: a safety net, not a fix

In Settings → Automatic improvements you can let Google update price and availability from your page when they differ. It cuts down disapprovals, and I usually turn it on. But it relies on your structured data being right, and it doesn't fix the cause. If the feed is wrong every day, Google is correcting you every day, and that's not a pattern you want on an account under review.

How to check a single product

  1. In Merchant Center open the product and copy the link and the feed price.
  2. Open the link in a private window with a VPN set to the target country. Compare the price, currency and stock status with the feed.
  3. Run the same URL through the Rich Results Test and look at offers.price, priceCurrency and availability.
  4. If all three agree and the product is still disapproved, wait for the next crawl or fetch the feed again manually.
