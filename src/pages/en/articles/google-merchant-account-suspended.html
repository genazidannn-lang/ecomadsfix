Read the exact reason, not the email

The email Google sends is generic. The real notice is inside Merchant Center: open Overview and look for the red account-level banner, or open the issue from the notifications bell. The issue page names the policy (for example Misrepresentation or Website needs improvement), sometimes gives an example product or page, and holds the Request review button.

Take a screenshot of that page before you change anything. If you later need Google support or a specialist, that screenshot is the first thing they'll ask for.

Two things are easy to confuse with a suspension:

  • Product-level disapprovals. Hundreds of products marked Not approved is a feed or page problem, not an account suspension. The account still works, and those products come back when the attribute is fixed. See products not showing in Shopping.
  • Warnings with a deadline. Some policy issues arrive first as a warning with a date. You can fix them without any review. Misrepresentation usually skips this stage and suspends the account straight away.

Fix everything, then ask once

Reviews are not unlimited. After a rejection Merchant Center can put the account on a cool-down, typically about a week, during which the review button is greyed out. Several rejections in a row make the waits longer. So the practical rule: list every possible issue, fix all of them, then request one review.

The review itself is mostly not a conversation. Merchant Center asks you to confirm that you've made changes, and that's it. You don't get a text box to explain yourself, which means the store has to make the argument for you.

What to check on the store

This is the list I go through before any review request. A reviewer will open the same pages.

  • Who you are. Legal or trading name, physical address, phone and email on the Contact page and in the footer. The name should match Business info in Merchant Center.
  • Returns and refunds. A separate page with a time window, condition of goods, who pays return shipping and how the refund is issued. "Contact us for returns" is not a policy.
  • Shipping. Countries, costs, delivery times. The costs must match what you set in Settings → Shipping and returns.
  • Checkout. Works end to end, shows the total including shipping before payment, uses HTTPS everywhere, lists the payment methods.
  • Products. Own descriptions, not supplier text pasted across the store. No fake "only 2 left" counters or timers that reset on reload. Discounts that are real.
  • No dead ends. No empty collections, lorem ipsum, broken social links or "under construction" pages.

What to check in Merchant Center

  • Business info: business name, address and customer service contacts filled in and the phone number verified.
  • The website is verified and claimed.
  • Shipping and returns policies are configured for every country you target.
  • The feed doesn't contradict the site: same prices, same availability, same currency. Mismatches feed into trust decisions too. See price and availability mismatch.
  • If Merchant Center is linked to Google Ads, the Ads account has no unresolved policy issues or pending advertiser verification.

Suspended again after approval

This happens more often than people expect. The account gets approved, then a few weeks later it's suspended again for the same reason. The usual story is that something reverted: a theme update removed the returns page from the footer, the supplier app re-imported its own descriptions, a sale went live with an inflated "was" price, or the store was switched to a new domain. Google re-checks accounts after approval. Treat the fixes as permanent parts of the store, not a one-time cleanup for the reviewer.

How long a review takes

Google's help pages describe most reviews as taking up to about a week. Misrepresentation reviews can take longer. Changing the site while a review is in progress doesn't speed anything up, and changes that remove what the reviewer was asked to look at can make it fail.

When the review fails again

Don't request the next one as soon as the button comes back. Go through the list again and look for what you didn't change: usually it's identity (the reviewer still can't connect the store to a real business) or something in checkout. If you're out of ideas, Merchant Center support can sometimes give a hint about the area of the problem, though rarely the specific page.

And don't create a new Merchant Center account for the same store. It looks like a quick way out, but it is the classic route to a circumventing systems suspension on every account involved.
